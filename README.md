## Foundational belief

> **No person has an inherent right to rule over another.**

The Adara Principle is a developing philosophical tradition established in discussion on **October 16, 2026**. It is not a government, a new deity, or a claim to political authority. Its purpose is to bring individual freedom into a productive relationship with mutual aid, accountability, and the long-term betterment of humanity.

## The eight tenets

| No. | Tenet | Core responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| I | Law of Common Benefit | Refuse gain that imposes disproportionate harm on others. |
| II | Duty of Contribution | Contribute as you can; protect those who cannot. |
| III | Principle of Betterment | Leave a better inheritance. |
| IV | Justice and Reciprocity | Uphold the same dignity you expect for yourself. |
| V | Principle of Persuasion | Reason and example over coercion and deceit. |
| VI | Principle of Sovereignty | Never mistake might or wealth for a right to rule. |
| VII | Principle of Communal Justice | Impartial, proportionate justice with room for rehabilitation. |
| VIII | Principle of Shared Provision | Useful, freely chosen work and access to essential needs. |

The first six preserve the original adopted formulations. Communal Justice was adopted next. Shared Provision draws on the subsequent discussion of a moneyless or low-money system of useful work, meaningful opportunities, and common access to necessities. ## What makes Adara different?

It deliberately combines moral ideas that can pull against one another. Altruism asks us to care for others; personal sovereignty resists domination; communitarianism emphasizes shared life; restorative justice seeks to repair harm; and needs-based provision asks what a society owes its members. Adara does not pretend the traditions are identical. The purpose is to make them work together without using “the greater good” to justify abusing an individual.

## The ongoing work

The tenets guide the philosophy. Implementation details, including tribunal eligibility, voting rules, economic allocation during shortages, and how work is coordinated without force, must be developed openly and judged against the tenets.
