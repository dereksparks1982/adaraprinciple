# The Eight Tenets

## Foundational belief

**No person has an inherent right to rule over another.** Adara is a voluntary ethical philosophy and not a government of compulsory obedience.

These tenets guide conduct toward other people, not religious worship or belief. They do not demand that anyone renounce God or their faith, nor do they treat Earth as a deity. Care for the world people inhabit serves the people who live here and those who will follow.

### I. The Law of Common Benefit

> No individual shall enrich themselves, pursue glory, or satisfy personal ambition through activities that impose disproportionate suffering, danger, or deprivation upon others.

Creating a job, or making money, does not by itself prove that something is worthwhile. The Everest example first raised the cost of status-seeking when others carry the danger. Ordinary pleasure and voluntary recreation are not automatically forbidden.

### II. The Duty of Contribution

> Every person who is able shall contribute to the welfare of civilization according to their abilities, and every person unable to contribute shall remain entitled to its protection.

Care work, teaching, childraising, cultivating food, and maintenance are contributions. Those who are ill, elderly, disabled, or otherwise unable must not lose the community's protection.

### III. The Principle of Betterment

> Every generation bears a responsibility to leave the world in better condition than it inherited.

Land, resources, institutions, useful knowledge, and traditions worth preserving belong to this long-term responsibility.

### IV. Justice and Reciprocity

> No person shall demand from others a sacrifice they would not willingly bear themselves, nor claim privileges that deny others their rightful dignity and opportunity.

No one, including elders, community decision-makers, and influential families, is exempt from the standards applied to everyone else.

### V. The Principle of Persuasion

> Seek to change minds through reason, example, and respectful dialogue, never through coercion, deception, or the demand for obedience.

Conviction is stronger than compelled obedience. Ethical persuasion must leave another person free to disagree.

### VI. The Principle of Sovereignty

> No individual possesses an inherent right to rule, exploit, dispossess, or subjugate another. Neither strength, wealth, influence, nor circumstance grants such authority. Protection is not ownership; leadership is not dominion.

A breakdown in civil order never turns theft, threats, or the violent seizure of others' possessions into legitimate leadership.

### VII. The Principle of Communal Justice

> No person shall stand above the justice of the community. Disputes and offenses shall be judged by impartial members of that community, according to established principles of fairness, evidence, and proportionality.
>
> Those who transgress shall be held accountable according to the severity of their actions, their intentions, and their history of offenses. Whenever reasonably possible, correction, restitution, and rehabilitation shall precede harsher punishment.

This tenet was **adopted**, not left in draft. Detailed procedures, such as jury size, eligible voters, the advisory role of elders, and appeal rules, are documented as continuing design questions in [JUSTICE.md](JUSTICE.md).

### VIII. The Principle of Shared Provision

> Each person should have a genuine opportunity to pursue useful work suited to their interests and abilities, and each person should have access to life's necessities according to need. The community shall coordinate its resources for shared well-being without treating anyone as property or making livelihood conditional on obedience.

This wording expresses the later discussion of shared grain, access to essentials without ordinary wages, opportunity to develop a beloved vocation, and limits on compulsory job assignment. A child who wants to become an astronaut should not encounter arbitrary barriers to learning or qualifying; however, public resources devoted to a profession must be weighed against community needs. The proposed Adara priority is **practical betterment of people living on Earth**, not prestige-driven spaceflight. This does not require ignoring terrestrial benefits that some space technology can provide.

See [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) for the model and unresolved practical questions.

## The working relationship

**Common Benefit** limits harm. **Contribution** defines mutual responsibility. **Betterment** broadens the time horizon. **Reciprocity** demands fairness. **Persuasion** protects independent thought. **Sovereignty** guards liberty. **Communal Justice** governs accountability. **Shared Provision** addresses material needs and meaningful work.

Together, they require that a claimed collective benefit never become a blank check to abuse a person.
